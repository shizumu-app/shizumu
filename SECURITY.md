# security policy

## Reporting a vulnerability

Email **hello@shizumu.app** with `SECURITY` in the subject line. If you prefer a
tracker, open a **confidential** issue at
<https://github.com/shizumu-app/shizumu/issues>.

Please do not open a public issue for a security problem before we have had a
chance to respond.

We aim to acknowledge a report within **72 hours** and to give you an assessment
— including whether we consider it in scope and a rough remediation timeline —
within **10 days**. If you have not heard back in that window, assume the mail
went astray and chase us; that is not a nuisance.

You will be credited in the release notes for the fix unless you ask not to be.

## What to include

- The affected file(s) and a minimal reproduction.
- Whether the issue is exploitable against a stock self-hosted relay, or only
  against the hosted service.
- The version or commit hash of the client and relay you tested.

## Scope

This repository is the shizumu client — a local-first desktop and mobile writing
app. The sync relay is a separate codebase:
<https://github.com/shizumu-app/shizumu-relay>. Reports for either are welcome at
the address above; say which one you mean.

**In scope**

- Anything that exposes note content to a party that should not see it. All
  data is local by default, and sync payloads are end-to-end encrypted — the
  relay stores ciphertext only and never holds a key that can decrypt it.
- Weaknesses in the sync cryptography: key exchange, device pairing, the
  pairing-phrase flow, key rotation, or the encrypted-blob format.
- Authentication or authorisation flaws in the relay, including anything that
  lets one user reach another user's blobs.
- Local privilege or sandbox escapes, including flatpak sandbox escapes.
- Attacks via untrusted note content — a crafted document that achieves code
  execution when opened or imported.

**Out of scope**

- Attacks requiring an already-compromised device. If an attacker has your
  machine, they have your notes; the threat model does not claim otherwise.
- The absence of a lock screen or at-rest encryption on the local database.
  This is a deliberate design decision, documented in the threat model.
- Denial of service against a relay you host yourself.
- Reports generated by automated scanners with no demonstrated impact.
- Missing hardening headers on the marketing site.

## Threat model

In short, so you can tell quickly whether a finding is in scope.

Your writing is encrypted on your device before it goes anywhere. The relay
stores ciphertext and the minimum index needed to work out which device is
missing which change. It never holds your keys, so a relay operator, a host
with disk access, or anyone who obtains a backup learns what size things are
and when they were written, and not what they say.

What is therefore in scope: anything that lets plaintext or key material
leave a device, anything that lets one account read another's data, and
anything that lets a relay operator recover content.

What is not: the metadata above, which is a stated property rather than a
flaw, and denial of service against a relay you host yourself.

## Disclosure

We would like to ship a fix before details are public. If you plan to publish,
tell us your intended date and we will do our best to have a release out first.
We will not ask you to delay indefinitely, and we will not threaten you for
reporting in good faith.
